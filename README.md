# 100 TSD Forms

A personal forms library and progress tracker, built around a long-term goal: learn 100 Tang Soo Do (and adjacent) forms.

## Background

I'm Victor Ronin, a second-degree black belt in Tang Soo Do, training in New York City under my instructor, Master Ralph. Our studio is part of the World Tang Soo Do Association. Along the way I discovered that I really enjoy learning new forms (hyung/poomsae/kata) — I memorize sequences quickly and can get them into decent shape. This project is about turning that interest into a long-term, trackable goal.

Forms can be refined indefinitely — some practitioners spend years polishing a single hyung — so the standard here is competence, not perfection: each form learned to a solid passing grade. When possible, I plan to have forms reviewed by qualified instructors from the associations or systems where they originate, for outside feedback and validation. These videos aren't instructional material or a reference standard; they're a record of my own training and progress.

## Goal

Learn 100 forms over time: Tang Soo Do forms as practiced across different associations, plus adjacent forms from related arts (Taekwondo, Karate, Kung Fu, etc.) that are relevant to or influence Tang Soo Do practice. That number may shift over time — the real goal is long-term consistency and progress.

## What this is

A single front page (`index.html`) that lists the forms, grouped by family (e.g. Pyung Ahn, Naihanchi, Chil Sung) or standalone, alongside an About section with the background above. Planned expansion: a dedicated page per form (or per family), each including:

- **History** — when the form was created and by whom
- **Associations** — which organizations practice this form (and any naming/variation differences between them)
- **Stats** — number of moves and counts, number of kihaps
- **Videos** — links to reference videos found online (since plenty of practitioners perform these better than I currently do), plus my own recordings as I learn each form

## Progress tracking

Each form has a status:

- **Not started** — haven't begun learning it yet
- **Learning** — currently working on it
- **Learned** — learned, no video of my own yet
- **Recorded** — learned and I have my own video

The site also shows an overall progress bar toward the 100-form goal, based on these statuses.
