History

One of the oldest Okinawan kata, brought over from China and passed to Master Itosu Yasutsune through his teacher Master Matsumura Sokon. The name is debated: read literally it means "internal divided conflict," though an older pronunciation, Nifanchin, has been translated as "iron horse," matching the kata's constant horse-stance, side-facing movement.

Originally one long form, it was later divided into three — Cho Dan, E Dan, and Sam Dan — most likely by Itosu himself, around the same period he created the Pyung Ahn series. Master Funakoshi Gichin renamed it Tekki ("iron horseman") when he brought it to Japan.
